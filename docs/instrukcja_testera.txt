GENESIS ERROR - playtest build
==============================

Thank you for testing! This is an early prototype: simple graphics, all text
in English. We want to find out whether the game itself is fun, not how it
looks.


1. RUNNING IT (Windows 10/11, 64-bit)
-------------------------------------

1. Unzip the archive anywhere (for example to the Desktop).
2. Run GenesisError.exe.
3. Windows may show "Windows protected your PC" (the game has no paid
   digital signature). Click "More info", then "Run anyway".

Nothing to install. The game does not connect to the internet.


2. WHAT IT IS ABOUT
-------------------

You are the Creator's Apprentice. Humanity has bet that any fool with divine
powers can create life. You get an empty planet and 200 years to prove them
wrong.

Life climbs a ladder on its own, one rung at a time, as conditions allow:
bacteria -> algae -> moss -> shrubs -> trees, and on the plants the animals:
insects (they live on moss) -> small animals (shrubs) -> large mammals
(trees). The bar over the globe shows the ladder, the next rung and what it
still needs.

GOAL: "Mature planet" - every stage of life, from bacteria to large mammals,
alive at the same time for 5 years. When you get there a card congratulates
you, and you can keep playing.

The planet has its own crises: droughts, ice ages, overheating, fire
seasons. They are announced before they strike (amber warning over the
globe, with the perks and actions that help). The first warning of each
kind stops the game and explains.


3. HOW TO PLAY
--------------

Start screen: type a planet number or leave it empty (random). The same
number is always the same planet, so you can replay it and try something
else. Planet character: Harmonious (calm), Chaotic (more crises), Guardian
(heals itself), or random.

The window:
  * the globe in the middle (drag to turn it, wheel to zoom). Over it:
    the ladder of life, the chronicle (what happened and why, in the
    translucent box; the "Chronicle" button shows all of it) and warnings.
    Bubbles float over the globe: CLICK THEM, they are Sparks of Life.
  * left: the planet's numbers and the life on it (arrows show what rose or
    fell over the last few years), and the planting guide
  * right: the "Perks" button, the divine actions, speed, pause, goals
  * top: the date ("Year 29, November"), your Sparks and the cheapest perk
    you could buy next

Planting guide: for each species a coloured dot says how well the planet
suits it right now (green = ideal, yellow = weak, red = too early, grey =
waiting for the species it grows from). Hover for what it needs and what the
planet has. "Plant" (plants) or "Release" (animals) puts a little of it on
the planet. Animals also appear on their own once their food has been
plentiful for long enough.

Perks (button, or press P): the game pauses while the perk window is open,
so you can read. Six branches: Environment (divine powers), Resistance (cold,
heat, drought, fire), Sowing (more per sowing, spreading by itself), Production
(growth, soil, oxygen), Shield (softer and rarer crises, at a price) and
Fauna (opens once insects have lived). Many perks come in levels (I, II,
III). Every perk has a price in the world (a side effect). You can refund a
perk for part of its cost.

Speed: x5 (slow) ... x25 (default) ... x100 (fast). Pause stops the planet.
The game saves itself regularly and when you close the window; "Load last
game" on the start screen continues it.

A game takes about 10-20 minutes, depending on the speed and how long you
read.


4. WHAT I WANT TO KNOW (the most important part!)
-------------------------------------------------

After 1-3 games please answer briefly:

 1. Did you know WHEN to plant shrubs and when trees? What told you?
 2. Did you notice the warning before a crisis? What did you do?
 3. Was the perk window clear? Did you have something to spend Sparks on
    all the time? Did different choices feel different?
 4. Were the animals a reward? Did you notice when they appeared?
 5. Did you always know what was happening between sowing moss and growing
    a tree, and what came next?
 6. Did you know when you had finished? Did the ending card feel good?
 7. Was the speed too slow, too fast, just right?
 8. Would you play again (another planet or the same one)?
 9. One thing you would change.

It helps a lot if you attach the chronicles of your games. They are text
files: type in the Windows Explorer address bar
    %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Genesis Error\logs\simulation_runs
and copy the *.chronicle.txt files (one per game).

Bugs and odd behaviour are valuable too: write what happened and give the
planet number (shown at the top of the window, "planet").
